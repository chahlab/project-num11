ISS Crew Wraps Up Major Solar Array Upgrade in Orbit

The seven Expedition 75 crew members aboard the International Space Station wrapped up a busy week of orbital maintenance and biomedical research following a nearly seven-hour spacewalk. Flight engineers Joshua Kutryk and Anil Menon finalized the installation of a new roll-out solar array, boosting the station's total power capacity from 160 to 215 kilowatts to support upcoming microgravity experiments.

How the Station Stays Powered in the Dark

Because the International Space Station orbits Earth every 90 minutes, it spends roughly 35 minutes of every loop in complete darkness. During the sunny half of each orbit, the upgraded solar arrays generate excess electricity to charge 24 heavy-duty lithium-ion battery packs mounted along the station's truss, keeping life-support systems and science labs running seamlessly until sunrise.
